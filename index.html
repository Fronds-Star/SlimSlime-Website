Introduction

SlimSlime is a creative internet site on making slime. Not just on making slime for fun, we can learn how the slime works and you may know that the matter of slime is an example of an non-Newtonian fluid. So you must know the basic chemistry in order to understand the structure of the slime! We can learn about that in this website too!.

And not just slimes we're making, we can make any mixture related to slimes like a half of a glass of water and twice a cup of cornstarch! We can learn the characteristics of its molecules here! But before we get started, I will give a simple definition and a short introduction of a slime!

So slime is a type of polymer, which is a large molecule formed by many repeating units of smaller molecules. There are naturally occurring polymers, including cellulose (found in wood and paper) and starch (found in plants), as well as human made polymers, such as polyester (used in fabrics) and polystyrene (used in foam cups). Polymers are also known as plastics.

Slime has properties of both solids and liquids. Like a solid, slime can be formed into a ball and keep its shape. However, slime contains over 90% water and can flow like a liquid. Sometime slime can be an oobleck mixture.

There are number of ways of making slime using different ingredients. Different ingredients means our slime has different qualities. Depending what kind of slime you want, all slimes has similar ingredients and that is a glue and a slime activator. There are numbers of slime activators but all of those activators must contain sodium tetraborate.
